## 2. Summary

The manuscript establishes that EFE minimization is a rho-POMDP with information-gain weight one and then reinterprets that weight as an operational shadow price of a sensing budget. On my lens the theory is correct and carefully scoped. The two-line information-floor argument (Section 4.1, line 411) is valid and its mirror image for the penalized CPOMDP reference (line 716) is valid for exact maximizers. The manuscript is consistent at every one of the eighteen places where it mentions Lagrange or Lagrangian (lines 83 through 1863) in saying that w is a multiplier of an information floor and not the multiplier of the usage cap, that the operational shadow price is a calibration and not lambda-star, and that the CPOMDP reference is near-optimal and estimated rather than exact. Proposition PI-1's proof is correct and its tie-break hypothesis is met by the implementation (`_strictly_better` in both agent files is homogeneous of degree one). Proposition PI-2's proof is the standard scalarization argument and is correct, and the manuscript is honest that it does not transfer to the receding-horizon count staircase. Corollary PI-4 follows from the H equals one indicator form of U and is numerically confirmed at 3.44 and 7.55 bits. Proposition PI-5 maps the projected recursion onto Robbins and Monro, Blum, and Kushner and Clark with the four conditions stated inside the proposition, and the reported runs use the projected decaying-step controller it covers (w_max equals 100, delta equals 0.02, eta_0 equals 0.05, maximum iterate 3.09). Definition PI-3 matches `solve_shadow_price_from_curve` and `crossing_bracket` exactly, including the last-crossing convention that the Bandit curve exercises. The feasible-envelope reference reproduces through `rho_aif.budget.feasible_envelope` at every budget I tried, and every quantitative claim I checked in the budget sections matches its CSV. The POMCP, SARSOP, and CPOMDP baselines are fairly constructed and their fidelity gaps and seeding conventions are disclosed with a precision that is rare. I found no blocking defect. The optional suggestions below are strengthenings, one of which (a free time-average corollary to Proposition PI-5) the authors may want to adopt.

## 3. Strengths

- The Lagrangian story is argued correctly and consistently. The manuscript distinguishes the usage-cap multiplier lambda, the information-floor multiplier w, and the operational shadow price w-star(B), proves the one identification it claims (w as floor multiplier for exact maximizers), and never slides between them.
- Definition PI-3 is a precise, four-object definition (level set, crossing threshold, grid bracket, endpoint mixture) that the released solver implements literally. I reproduced the Bandit brackets (1.64, 3.73], (0.06, 0.14], and (3.73, 8.48] by calling the library on the committed calibration curve.
- Proposition PI-1 is a theorem about the implemented agent, not a heuristic, and the tie-break that makes it hold is in the code with a docstring explaining why an absolute epsilon would break it. The bit-exact collapse in Table 3 is exactly what the theorem predicts.
- The CPOMDP reference is built the right way for the problem class: a Lagrangian sweep over SARSOP, with the duality-gap caveat stated correctly for the continuous belief MDP, and the envelope taken as an LP over sampled points with usage at most B rather than an interpolation at equal usage.
- The budget-frontier study is predeclared, uses held-out seeds, declares unattainable budgets, and reports a result that is unflattering to the method (eleven of twelve budgets below the envelope). The two policies added after inspection are disclosed as such in the producer's docstring and in the text.
- The dual-control comparison uses a restricted estimand with a paired interval, discloses the one non-recovering seed, and its numbers (157.3 versus 51.2, difference 106.1 with interval [96.8, 115.4], minimum paired difference 89, ratio 3.07) recompute exactly from `results_price_dual_multiseed_metrics.csv`.
- The POMCP appendix states three fidelity gaps that I confirmed in `pomcp.py` (rollouts start from the root belief, the depth-cap leaf uses the sampled state, the rollout credits a belief-expected commit), says which direction each cuts, seeds the planner stream per run, and selects constants on disjoint tuning seeds under a rule recorded before selection while calling the Tiger result retrospective.

## 5. Optional suggestions

1. Section 4.5, Proposition PI-5, line 497, "neither the expected usage at $w_t$ nor its time average is guaranteed to converge to $B$". The time-average half of this sentence is an under-claim. Under the proposition's own hypotheses w_t converges to an interior w-star, so the projection is eventually inactive and the partial sums of a_t (B minus U_t) converge. With a_t equal to (eta_0 over delta) divided by (t plus one over delta), Kronecker's lemma then gives (1 over n) times the sum of U_t converging to B almost surely, at gap budgets included. This is a one-line corollary that would give the dual controller an exact time-average budget-attainment property and would anchor the rolling-mean recovery criterion of Section 5.4 in the theory. Suggested fix: replace the clause with "the expected usage at $w_t$ is not guaranteed to converge to $B$, although its time average is, by Kronecker's lemma applied to the convergent increments" and add the two-line argument after the proof paragraph.

2. Section 5.9, line 754, "compare estimates from different evaluation streams, so a held-out reward above the reference's sample mean is not evidence". The disclosure is correct but the size of the stream effect is not shown. On Tiger the reference envelope (5.02, canonical seeds, once-per-seed seeding, 300 episodes) and the held-out best feasible member (5.68, seeds 7 to 11, per-episode seeding, 100 episodes) are the same policy up to the tie at w equals 0 that Table 5 records, and the 0.67 difference exceeds every reported Tiger and Bandit gap to the envelope except Bandit at 10.07. The saved SARSOP-Lagrangian policies could be re-evaluated on the held-out seeds under per-episode seeding at no solver cost, which would put the reference and the family on one stream and allow a paired standard error on each gap. Suggested fix: run that evaluation and report the envelope from it, or at minimum add one sentence giving the Tiger 5.02 versus 5.68 figure as the measured size of the stream effect.

3. Section 4.6, line 511, "it can only estimate the constrained optimum from below, up to its own sampling error", and Section 5.7, line 739, "a negative estimated gap indicates reference-frontier noise". The envelope takes a maximum over about twelve noisy sampled points, so the estimate is subject to upward selection bias as well as sampling error. `results_cpomdp_frontier.csv` shows this concretely: on Diagnosis the penalized points at lambda 0.05 and 0.107 both out-score the unpenalized lambda equals 0 point (minus 1.265 and minus 1.297 against minus 1.587, standard errors 0.31 to 0.40), which an exact sweep could not produce, and the envelope selects the lucky one. Section 5.9 already says "the selection of the estimated envelope" at line 754. Suggested fix: carry that phrase into lines 511 and 739 so the from-below statement is qualified by selection as well as by sampling error.

4. Table 6 caption, line 724, "$w^*{=}0.00$, $0.45$, and $1.07$ for Tiger, Diagnosis, and Bandit respectively". These are the nearest-grid-point weights returned by `planning_ig_at_budget` from a lighter usage curve (episodes divided by five, `tol=0.2`, ties to the smaller weight), not crossing brackets, and the manuscript's convention is that shadow prices are reported as brackets. They are legitimately the weights that were run, and the bit-identity with EFE has a mechanical explanation the caption does not give: each lies on the same usage plateau as w equals 1 (Diagnosis [0.316, 19.3], Bandit [0.72, 1.64], Tiger [0, 19.3]), so it is the same policy. Suggested fix: call them "the usage-matched grid weights actually run" and add that each shares a usage plateau with w equals 1, which is why the rewards are bit-identical.

5. Section 4.1, line 409, "Solving $U(w) = B$ for $w$ gives the operational shadow price $w^*(B)$". Ten lines later Definition PI-3 makes w-star(B) the grid bracket and says the level set can be empty, so the equation has no solution at a gap budget. Suggested fix: "Solving $U(w) = B$ for $w$, in the bracketed sense that Definition PI-3 fixes, gives the operational shadow price $w^*(B)$".

6. Definition PI-3, line 419, "as it also does when the final grid point falls back below $B$". The crossing threshold as defined (a supremum over weights below B that precede a weight at or above B) exists for such a curve, while the solver returns an unbracketed fallback because it requires the last sub-budget grid weight to be followed by a grid weight at or above B. The manuscript says no swept curve has this shape, which I confirmed on every curve CSV, so nothing reported is affected. Suggested fix: one clause noting that the solver's bracket is defined only when the curve ends at or above B, a stricter condition than the threshold's existence.

7. Code vocabulary. `experiments/run_cpomdp_baseline.py` lines 12 to 24 describe the reference as "a strong-duality Lagrangian relaxation" and as measuring the gap "relative to the true optimal policy", and `rho_aif/budget.py` line 302 calls the bracket a "Set-valued shadow price". The manuscript itself explicitly declines all three phrasings (line 718 declines strong duality for the belief MDP, line 716 calls the reference near-optimal, Definition PI-3 says the bracket is not a set of exact prices). Suggested fix: align the two docstrings with the manuscript's calibrated wording.
