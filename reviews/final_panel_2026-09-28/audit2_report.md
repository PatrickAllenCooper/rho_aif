1. **Definition PI-3 and notation.** The revised definition now consistently makes the operational shadow price \(w^*(B)\) the last crossing threshold \(w^\dagger(B)\), the half-open interval \((w_{\mathrm{lo}},w_{\mathrm{hi}}]\) its finite-grid estimate, and \(\hat w(B)\) the nearest grid point minimizing \(|U-B|\). The introductory glosses “the weight at which expected sensing usage crosses \(B\)” and “the weight at which the family's sensing passes from below \(B\) to at or above it” agree with that convention. The final-grid fallback qualification correctly distinguishes existence of a continuous-curve threshold from the solver's stricter requirement that the sampled grid contain a straddling pair after the last below-budget point. The staircase captions correctly identify plotted markers as \(\hat w(B)\), including markers on a bracket's excluded lower endpoint and slack markers below it.

2. **Scale equivariance.** Proposition PI-1 gives
   \(U_{\mathrm{count},\alpha}(\alpha w)=U_{\mathrm{count}}(w)\) and
   \(U_{\mathrm{cost},\alpha}(\alpha w)=\alpha U_{\mathrm{cost}}(w)\).
   Substituting into the last-crossing definition yields
   \(w^*_{\mathrm{count}}(B;\alpha)=\alpha w^*_{\mathrm{count}}(B;1)\) without a grid condition and
   \(w^*_{\mathrm{cost}}(B;\alpha)=\alpha w^*_{\mathrm{cost}}(B/\alpha;1)\) for a numerically fixed cost budget. A grid bracket scales only when the grid scales, exactly as the revised paragraph states. The fixed-cost-budget and co-scaled-cost-budget cases are kept separate.

3. **Projected dual-controller theorem and running average.** Under the proposition's sign, separation, bounded-noise, independence, and Robbins--Monro step-size conditions, the cited Blum conditions apply without requiring continuity of the regression function. Projection onto an interval containing the interior crossing is non-expansive relative to the crossing, so the scalar almost-sure convergence argument survives projection. Once \(w_t\to w^*\), bounded usage and \(a_t\to0\) make projection inactive eventually. The unprojected recursion then gives convergence of \(\sum_t a_t(B-U_t)\). Kronecker's lemma with \(1/a_t=(1+\delta t)/\eta_0\), followed by \(n a_n\to\eta_0/\delta\), correctly yields
   \[
   n^{-1}\sum_{t<n}U_t\to B
   \]
   almost surely, including at a usage gap. The manuscript correctly distinguishes this time average from \(U(w^*)\) and from the stationary endpoint mixture. The result is explicitly limited to the harmonic schedule, while the point-convergence proposition states the broader square-summable/non-summable step-size conditions.
