## 2. Summary

The paper separates four objects that are routinely conflated when an information-gain weight is "tuned to a budget": the level set of the usage curve, the population crossing threshold (which it calls the operational shadow price), the finite-grid bracket that estimates it, and the per-episode endpoint mixture that attains a gap budget in expectation. It proves exact scale equivariance for the receding-horizon Planning+IG family (a one-line homogeneity argument, correctly hedged by a relative tie tolerance), monotone comparative statics for exact maximizers (standard, and correctly disclaimed for observation counts and receding-horizon agents), and almost-sure convergence of a projected Robbins-Monro controller to a single sign-consistent crossing, including discontinuous curves, with a Kronecker-lemma running-average corollary. It then shows that the recursive reward-based EFE objective is a rho-POMDP with information-gain weight one, exact under log scoring and conventional otherwise, and extends the identity to hidden-state-preserving actions in factored POMDPs, with a sharp destructive-sensing counterexample.

Empirically, the calibration procedure meets held-out expected-usage targets to within 0.13 observations on all twelve attainable targets, while the paper measures and reports the reward cost of doing so against (a) SARSOP as an unconstrained reference, (b) a feasible envelope over SARSOP-Lagrangian policies as a cap-constrained reference, and (c) LP-selected mixtures within the family. POMCP is a disclosed-weak baseline whose exploration constant and rollout policy are swept, and the paper repeatedly declines to claim superiority over state-of-the-art POMDP solvers.

## 3. Strengths

- The distinction between an expected-usage target and a usage cap is stated at the outset, carried through every comparison, and illustrated with a schematic that correctly shows why an endpoint mixture need not be the best mixture at the target. The paper never lets w drift into being read as the Lagrange multiplier of the cap, and the information-floor relaxation argument in Section 4.1 is correct as written.
- The constrained reference is defined once (`rho_aif.budget.feasible_envelope`) and used everywhere. It is a two-constraint LP, so the at-most-two-support invariant that the code asserts is right, and the main text states that fact correctly.
- Stream control in the held-out study is handled properly. The original cross-stream gap on Tiger (+0.34) reverses to a paired same-stream gap (-0.33 +- 0.04) once the reference is re-evaluated with the family's per-episode seeding, and the paper leads with the paired result. The Tiger rare-event sensitivity calculation (0.6 percent wrong-commit rate times 110 reward equals about 0.66) is exactly the kind of check a POMDP referee would otherwise have to do.
- Proposition PI-5 is proved directly rather than by citation, condition (iii) is correctly weakened to not require U(w*) = B, and the text is careful that at a gap budget the weight converges while the expected usage does not.
- The POMCP fidelity gaps (clairvoyant depth-cap leaf, belief-expected rollout commit) are named before the numbers, and the direction of each bias is stated. Both are indeed favorable or neutral to POMCP, so the gap they qualify is not an artifact of a handicapped baseline.
- Every number I spot-checked matched its CSV at the stated precision (Section 6).

## 4. Required changes

### R1. Attribute the Diagnosis interior shortfalls correctly

Current text, JAIR source lines 671 and 681:

> "Meeting a target can buy useful information or impose an avoidable reward cost. On Diagnosis at $B{=}7.72$, $9.53$, and its gap target $7.84$, the mixture earns $-2.14$, $-2.11$, and $-2.06$ against the feasible member's $-2.86$."

> "This study supports expected-usage calibration within the tested family, including gap targets, while quantifying its limits as a reward optimizer."

What is wrong. Table `tab:budget_frontier_main` reports negative paired gaps at Diagnosis B = 9.53 (-0.76 +- 0.15) and B = 11.35 (-1.10 +- 0.22), and the paragraph frames shortfalls as a cost of meeting the target. On these two rows that framing does not follow from the CSVs. At B = 9.53 the selected mixture is 94 percent the w = 0.316 policy, which lies on the same usage plateau as w = 1 (calibration usage 9.772 for every grid weight from 0.316 to 19.3, `results_budget_frontier_curve.csv`), and the held-out reference at that budget is 91 percent the unpenalized SARSOP policy. The shortfall is therefore essentially the w = 1 Planning+IG planner trailing SARSOP at matched usage on this stream, not a price paid for spending more. `results_budget_frontier_heldout_reference.csv` makes this explicit: the plateau policy alone (`endpoint_lo` at B = 11.35, `endpoint_hi` at B = 9.53) has paired gap -1.01 +- 0.18 and -0.86 +- 0.18 against the lam = 0 reference, at usage 9.70 versus 9.89.

That same policy pair, EFE at w = 1 versus unpenalized SARSOP on Diagnosis, is declared TOST-equivalent within +-1.0 in Section 6.6 (five-seed p = 0.046, twenty-seed difference +0.15 +- 0.14, `results_tost_sarsop_n20_robustness.csv`) and shows a gap of -0.04 at B_EFE in Table `tab:cpomdp`. The paper's own fresh-seed replication (`results_budget_frontier_fresh_seed_replication.csv`) sides with the equivalence: at B = 9.53 the fresh-seed paired gap is +0.02 +- 0.29. So the held-out Diagnosis interior rows are a 500-episode stream on which a heavy-tailed (-50 penalty) difference came out at the edge of the equivalence margin, and the text currently lets a reader attribute that to calibration.

Concrete replacement. After the sentence ending "against the feasible member's $-2.86$." on line 671, insert:

> "The Diagnosis shortfalls at $B{=}9.53$ and $11.35$ are of a different kind. There the selected mixture is dominated by the $w{=}0.316$ plateau, which is the $w{=}1$ policy's usage plateau, and the reference is dominated by the unpenalized SARSOP policy at nearly the same usage ($9.70$ against $9.89$). The paired gap for that plateau policy alone is $-1.01 \pm 0.18$ on this stream, so these rows measure the finite-horizon planner against the offline reference at matched usage rather than a cost of meeting the target. Section~\ref{sec:sarsop}'s twenty-seed estimate of the same comparison is $+0.15 \pm 0.14$, and the fresh-seed replication at $B{=}9.53$ gives $+0.02 \pm 0.29$, so we read the held-out Diagnosis interior shortfalls as stream-specific."

Also change line 681's "while quantifying its limits as a reward optimizer" to "while quantifying its limits as a reward optimizer and separating, on Diagnosis, a planner-versus-reference gap from the cost of the target itself." If main-body length is a concern, the second sentence of the insert (the usage numbers) can move to Appendix `app:frontier_details` and be cited.

Why this blocks. Without it, Section 6.8 reads as evidence that calibration costs about one observation's worth of reward on Diagnosis at mid-range targets, while Sections 6.6 and 6.7 say the same policy at the same usage is reward-equivalent to the reference. Both cannot be the headline reading of the same policy pair, and the CSVs support the equivalence reading as the better-powered one.

## 5. Optional suggestions

- Appendix `app:pomcp`, line 1561: "exact EFE also differs from this POMCP in its exact beliefs, exact tree enumeration, and closed-form leaf values." The POMCP here samples root particles from the exact Bayesian belief (`BaseAgent.belief`) and, in the reported `rollout_belief="path"` mode, updates the rollout belief by exact Bayes along the simulated history. The difference is in value estimation (Monte Carlo averages versus exact enumeration) rather than belief representation. Suggest "its exact tree enumeration and closed-form leaf and commit values" and drop "exact beliefs" from this list. The module docstring's "Particle-based belief representation at each tree node" is likewise stale, since `child.particles` is appended and never read.
- POMCP's 30 percent per-step rollout termination with a belief-optimal commit is a reasonable semi-informed rollout, but on Tiger it makes shallow observe branches look bad for a reason unrelated to tree search (an early random commit at a weak posterior costs -100 with high probability). A rollout that commits only when the rollout belief clears a confidence threshold is the natural next-strongest baseline. The paper's disclaimers already cover this, so I list it only as the experiment a POMCP partisan would ask for.
- Section 6.8, line 677: "A predeclared replication on ten fresh seeds again excludes zero at eight of eleven distinct budgets, but changes which Diagnosis budgets fall short." It would help the reader to say which: the fresh seeds drop the B = 9.53 and gap shortfalls and add B = 7.72. This is one clause.
- Definition PI-3 is long for a definition environment. The sentence about the achievability tolerance (max(0.3, 0.1B)) is an implementation detail that could live in Section 4.5 beside the grid description.

**Decision-theoretic reading.** The target-matched reference is the right comparator for the question the paper asks. The cap envelope was flat at the unpenalized optimum at seven of eleven budgets, so a shortfall there necessarily bundled the cost of spending B with any planner inefficiency. Subsidized policies extend the sampled frontier above the unpenalized usage so that an equal-usage mixture exists at every tested target, and against it the family is within noise everywhere except Bandit's gap budget, which the paper already attributes to the endpoint procedure not selecting the best in-family mixture (the LP mixture earns 5.94 against 5.62). The paper keeps both references, labels the second predeclared and the usage-matched variant post hoc, and retains the selection-bias caveat. That is the honest presentation.
