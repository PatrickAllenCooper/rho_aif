Pricing the Sensing Budget in 𝜌-POMDPs,
with Expected Free Energy as the Canonical Information Weight
PATRICK COOPER, Department of Computer Science, University of Colorado Boulder, USA
ALVARO VELASQUEZ, Department of Computer Science, University of Colorado Boulder, USA
Background: Information-gain utilities make sensing valuable directly, but introduce a weight that is usually tuned per
task. An engineer may instead need to specify expected sensing usage. Objectives: We calibrate the information weight of a
𝜌-POMDP policy family to a sensing target and identify the role of Expected Free Energy (EFE) within that family. Methods:
We define an operational shadow price as a crossing threshold of the usage curve, distinguish it from a finite-grid bracket, and
construct an endpoint mixture for targets inside usage gaps. We prove scale equivariance of the receding-horizon planner and
stationary convergence of a projected controller under stated conditions. Minimizing the specified recursive, reward-based
EFE objective is equivalent to maximizing reward plus information gain at 𝑤 = 1 for observe-then-commit problems and
hidden-state-preserving actions in a factored extension. The reward–information identification is exact under log scoring and
otherwise depends on the declared reward convention. Results: Usage curves collapse after rescaling, and the controller
re-adapts empirically after a tenfold reward shift. Targets selected by a predeclared rule from calibration curves are met on
held-out seeds to within 0.13 observations. Calibration can nevertheless sacrifice reward relative to spending less or choosing
another mixture. Comparisons against usage-capped and target-matched references distinguish the cost of extra sensing from
shortfalls within the family. On real sensor records with learned likelihoods, two independently fixed targets transfer within
the represented collection period, one lies below the sampled range, and later batches defeat frozen calibration. Direct-penalty
and fixed-count policies achieve better classification accuracy. Conclusions: Usage calibration is appropriate when expected
sensing usage is the design requirement. It does not maximize reward under a cap. EFE supplies an information-unit anchor
under specified assumptions, while usage, reward, and success must be evaluated separately.

1    Introduction
An engineer designing an inspection policy may need to specify how much testing the system should perform
before deciding whether to repair or release an item. A standard partially observable Markov decision process
(POMDP) values a test through its effect on expected task reward. A 𝜌-POMDP additionally rewards properties
of the agent’s belief, allowing information gain to have explicit value (Araya-López et al. 2010). This introduces a
practical choice: how much weight should information gain receive relative to reward?
   We study a different design input, an expected sensing-usage target 𝐵. Such a target is useful when sensing is
itself a deliverable, as in a coverage, monitoring, or verification requirement. It specifies the average number of
tests or their expected cumulative cost. It is neither a hard limit on each episode nor an instruction to maximize

       POMDP planning and belief-dependent rewards. POMDPs formalize decision-making under state uncertainty

     (Kaelbling et al. 1998; Smallwood and Sondik 1973). Point-based offline solvers approximate the value function

95    2013; Smith and Simmons 2004). Online solvers plan from the current belief. POMCP uses Monte Carlo tree
96    search with UCB1 action selection and rollout evaluation (Silver and Veness 2010), DESPOT searches regularized
97    sparse belief trees (Ye et al. 2017), and POMCPOW uses progressive widening for continuous spaces (Sunberg and
98    Kochenderfer 2018). Value of Information Monte Carlo planning selectively skips observation branches with low
99    expected decision value (Laouar et al. 2026). Our planner instead adds expected information gain to the objective.
100   These are different uses of information within planning, and our comparisons do not establish superiority over
101   state-of-the-art POMDP solvers generally.
102      Araya-López et al. (2010) introduced 𝜌-POMDPs with belief-dependent rewards. Their 𝜌 denotes the whole
103   reward, whereas we reserve it for an information term added to the ordinary task reward. Convex belief rewards
104   support convex value functions and piecewise-linear convex approximations. Expected information gain is
105   concave in the belief, so that machinery does not apply directly. Fehr et al. (2018) treat non-convex rewards under
106   a Lipschitz hypothesis, and Benchetrit et al. (2025) develop an anytime planner for continuous spaces. For a finite
107   observation model, strictly positive likelihoods make expected information gain Lipschitz on the belief simplex.
108   Zero likelihoods, including noiseless sensing, can violate this condition. Our exact tree recursion does not rely on
109   that approximation guarantee.
110      POMDPs with information rewards were developed for active cooperative perception (Spaan et al. 2015), and
111   their relation to 𝜌-POMDPs is discussed by Satsangi et al. (2018). Earlier preference-elicitation work selected
112   queries by sequential value of information while retaining state-dependent rewards (Boutilier 2002). These
113   approaches establish the motivation for valuing information explicitly. Our focus is the coefficient on that value
114   and its relation to expected sensing usage.
115
         Information value and experimental design. Classical value of information measures the improvement an
116
      observation can bring to a decision (Howard 1966). Bayesian experimental design instead commonly selects
117
      observations by expected information gain (Foster et al. 2021; Lindley 1956). The criteria coincide under a
118
      particular reward model: when the reward is a proper log score, the expected improvement in score is the
119
      expected information gain (Bernardo 1979). This supplies the exact reward–information identification used here.
120
      Sequential experimental design is also the closest structural analogue to our observe-then-commit setting.
121
         Dynamic feature acquisition can greedily select features by conditional mutual information, with learned
122
      approximations to the required distributions (Covert et al. 2023). Our sensor-record study uses this established
123
      greedy rule as a fixed-count comparator. Its purpose is to test usage calibration within an information-weighted
124
      family, not introduce a new feature-selection principle.
125
         Information-directed sampling (IDS) trades instantaneous regret against information gain through their ratio,
126
      rather than through a fixed additive coefficient (Russo and Van Roy 2014). Walraven et al. (2025) connect active
127
      inference with information gathering in POMDPs. Our contribution within this setting is to define and evaluate a
128
      calibration procedure for the weight in a specified 𝜌-POMDP policy family.

130      Active inference and the EFE formulation. Active inference casts perception and action as variational inference (K.
131   Friston 2010; Parr, Pezzulo, et al. 2022). EFE combines pragmatic and epistemic terms (Da Costa, Parr, et al. 2020; K.

139   restrictions those roots impose on prior preferences. de Vries et al. (2025) derive EFE-based planning from a
140   model augmented with preference and epistemic priors. The generalised free energy of Parr and K. J. Friston

      4.6        Positioning against Prior Art
593   Resource-price adaptation is classical in constrained MDPs (Altman 1999), rational inattention (Matějka and
594   McKay 2015; Sims 2003), and SAC (Haarnoja, Zhou, Hartikainen, et al. 2018). Here one information weight
595   controls sensing usage across episodes. It differs from an information constraint internal to EFE (Kouw 2026) and
596   per-node constrained-POMDP dual ascent (Stocco et al. 2024). The contribution is the calibration procedure for
597   this policy family, with count/cost equivariance, grid crossings, endpoint mixtures and conditional stationary
598   convergence.
599      A constrained-POMDP solver may randomize belief-dependently and outperform a mixture that fixes one
600   weight for each episode (Kim et al. 2011). Our references use sampled SARSOP policies and estimated feasibility,
601   with approximation, sampling and selection uncertainty. The shallow RockSample reference does not reach EFE’s
602   operating region (Appendix H.7).
603      Offline calibration requires a weight sweep. Its benefit is a requirement stated in sensing units, rather than
604   reduced sampling. Under its hypotheses, the online controller can find a crossing without a sweep. Scale equiv-
605   ariance also transfers directly tuned weights on a correspondingly rescaled grid, so that benefit is not unique to
606   usage calibration.

819   To determine whether 𝑤=1 is reward-competitive at its natural operating point, we compare EFE with SARSOP
820   (Kurniawati et al. 2008) on Tiger, Diagnosis, and Bandit. The original APPL solver is built from source, each
821   environment is exported through a common model path, and the solve uses precision 10−3 at 𝛾=0.999. The
822   returned alpha-vector policy is evaluated in the same simulator as EFE at five seeds and 500 episodes per seed.
823   SARSOP is a near-optimal reference, not a certified exact solution to the reported Monte Carlo comparison. Build
824   and export details appear in Appendix G.1.
825      Fixed-margin two one-sided tests (TOST) (Schuirmann 1987) assess reward equivalence within one observation’s
826   cost, ±1.0 on Tiger and Diagnosis and ±0.5 on Bandit. The five-seed unpaired Welch TOST rejects both nulls on
827   all three environments after Holm correction. Its margins were chosen after a canonical-seed comparison was
828   inspected, so it is retrospective. The fifteen additional seeds in the 𝑛 = 20 check were fixed after the margin and
829   before that study ran, and all three equivalence conclusions persist.
830      A predeclared twenty-seed check also reseeds every episode, matching hidden states and observation draws
831   while actions agree. It retains equivalence, with paired reward differences +0.11±0.09 on Diagnosis and +0.04±0.03
832   on Bandit (seed-level SE). Tiger’s policies act identically. Appendix J.2 gives the numerical tests, intervals and
833   archives. These results establish equivalence within the stated margins on these three environments, not universal
834   near-optimality of 𝑤 = 1.

1010   conditional independence of sensors given gas identity, use one-observation lookahead, and cannot reacquire a

1068   6.10                                    Reward-Irrelevant Sensing
1069   An expected sensing target controls the amount of sensing, but does not ensure that it concerns the task. We
1070   test this limit with Distractor Diagnosis, whose hidden state combines the usual four-way condition with an
1071   independent binary nuisance bit. Commit rewards depend only on the condition. An added test observes only
1072   the nuisance bit, while the original tests observe only the condition. Under the independent generative process
1073   the belief stays factored, so the distractor carries exactly zero information about the condition. Appendix G.2
1074   states and tests that property, whose scope excludes correlated priors introduced through misspecification.
1075      We sweep ordinary Planning+IG over sixteen weights with five seeds and 100 episodes per weight and seed,
1076   refining the grid where distractor use begins. The distractor fraction is zero through 𝑤=3.5, 0.103 ± 0.001 at 𝑤=4,
1077   0.233 ± 0.003 at 𝑤=5, and approximately one third at the largest weights (Figure 7). Thus the observed onset
1078   bracket is (3.5, 4.0]. The canonical 𝑤=1 agent avoids the distractor on this instance because it is below onset,
1079   not because its objective is structurally immune. At sufficiently high weights, genuine information about the
1080   nuisance bit earns more epistemic value than the test costs.

       8     Discussion
1404
          Choosing a sensing requirement. Expected usage is an interpretable design input when sensing itself is required.
1405
       It controls neither reward efficiency nor relevance. The endpoint mixture can meet a target while earning less
1406
       than another mixture at that usage, or than a policy spending less. Ordinary information gain can also pay
1407
       for irrelevant uncertainty. Reward-equivalence scoring removes distractor spending in the tested factorized
1408
       benchmark, but does not prevent over-observation (Section 6.10). The simulated targets are calibration-derived.
1409
1410
                                                  Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research, Vol. 1, Article 1. Publication date: August 2026.
       1:32   •   Cooper and Velasquez


1411   The sensor-record targets were fixed independently of usage curves, but are researcher-defined rather than
1412   application requirements. Their later-batch failures require a fresh transfer check before using a frozen calibration.
1413
1414      Interpreting the information-unit default. The recursive EFE identity fixes 𝑤 = 1 after reward and information
1415   units are declared. That weight belongs to the level set of 𝑈 (1), but need not equal its crossing threshold.
1416   Its usefulness is empirical: it lies in the reward-maximizing run of sampled weights on three of five swept
1417   environments and is beaten on two. Larger weights can buy greater success at a reward cost. Reward, success
1418   and usage therefore require separate evaluation.
1419
          Observation structure and planning depth. The advantage depends on sensing opportunities that the search can
1420
       represent. State-space size and sensor count alone do not predict it. The largest Tileworld separation is against a
1421
       fixed-horizon policy that takes no scans. On RockSample[11,11] the weighted family gains nothing over Planning,
1422
       and the standalone heuristic beats the tree-search agents on the two larger instances. Navigation supplies another
1423
       negative case, with no epistemic agent significantly beating the greedy mover at any tested size (Appendix D.1).
1424
       These results do not imply superiority over reward-only planning at arbitrary depth.
1425
          At one-step depth, the recursion is myopic reward plus information gain at 𝑤 = 1. Horizon, discounting and
1426
       model accuracy affect its behavior. The finite-horizon comparisons above and the controls in Appendices D.2
1427
       and D.3 show these dependencies. The observed discount threshold is not a general regime boundary. Every
1428
       tested Tiger sensor mismatch affects EFE and Planning identically, providing no EFE-specific robustness benefit.
1429
       Overestimated sensor accuracy can cause premature commitment.
1430
1431      What the search controls establish. MCTS-EFE addresses the cost of exact belief-tree enumeration. Its main
1432   POMCP comparison matches simulation counts with different costs per simulation, and a separate Tiger control
1433   matches computation. Tuning both solvers on disjoint seeds leaves MCTS-EFE ahead on Tiger reward and
1434   success. The Diagnosis and Tileworld gaps also survive the tested exploration constants and information-gain
1435   rollouts. However, neither in-tree information gain nor max-backup has a detectable success or reward effect
1436   after multiplicity correction at five seeds. EFE leaf evaluation, exact beliefs and exact commit-child values remain
1437   coupled, so the experiments do not isolate directed observation selection or establish general solver superiority
1438   (Appendices L and F.2).
1439
1440      Structural and practical limits. The reduction requires hidden-state-preserving observation and navigation
1441   actions. State-changing sensing needs transition-aware predictive and posterior beliefs. The diagnostic Δ𝑇 is
1442   neither a proved additive correction nor a decision-error bound, and perfect sensing can make it infinite after
1443   arbitrarily small drift (Example H.2, Appendix H). The simulators use known models, and Structural Inspection
1444   uses a synthetic task and a hand-coded leaf rule. The sensor-record study learns a model, but its shallow planner
1445   and conditional-independence approximation establish no general acquisition advantage. More efficient search,
1446   transition-aware budget control and validation against an operational sensing requirement remain open.
1447
1448   9      Conclusion
1449   An expected sensing-usage requirement can calibrate the information-gain weight of a 𝜌-POMDP policy family.
1450   The operational shadow price is a crossing threshold, estimated by a grid bracket. Under stated conditions, mixing
1451   the bracket’s endpoint policies meets a usage-gap target in expectation. The receding-horizon planner is scale
1452   equivariant, and a projected decaying-step controller has a stationary-convergence guarantee under explicit
1453   hypotheses. Re-adaptation after reward-scale shifts is an empirical result, including a useful reset heuristic that
1454   the theorem does not cover.
1455     The simulated held-out study shows accurate usage calibration but also its reward cost. Real sensor records
1456   extend the test to learned likelihoods and targets fixed independently of usage curves. Two targets transfer within
1457
       Journal of Artificial Intelligence Research, Vol. 1, Article 1. Publication date: August 2026.
                                                                                                        Pricing the Sensing Budget in 𝜌-POMDPs,
                                                                         with Expected Free Energy as the Canonical Information Weight • 1:33


1458
       the represented collection period, one lies below the sampled range, and later batches defeat frozen calibration.
1459
       Direct cost tuning and fixed-count information acquisition also give better test accuracy in that configuration.
1460
       Calibration is therefore a way to adapt an already-selected information-weighted family to a feasible expected-use
1461
       target, subject to a transfer check. It is not a reason to prefer that family when directly counting or penalizing
1462
       accesses meets the design need.
1463
          The recursive EFE objective supplies an information-unit anchor at 𝑤 = 1, with exact reward–information
1464
       identification under log scoring and an explicit convention for ordinary rewards. This weight is competitive in
1465
       several benchmarks and suboptimal in others. Its practical value depends on the observation structure, planning
1466
       depth, and model assumptions. The resulting design procedure is to state the intended usage, calibrate within a
1467
       specified policy family, and assess usage, reward, and success together.
