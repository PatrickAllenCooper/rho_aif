Pricing the Sensing Budget in 𝜌-POMDPs,
with Expected Free Energy as the Canonical Information Weight
PATRICK COOPER, Department of Computer Science, University of Colorado Boulder, USA
ALVARO VELASQUEZ, Department of Computer Science, University of Colorado Boulder, USA
Background: Information-gain utilities make sensing valuable directly, but introduce a weight that is usually tuned per
task. An engineer may instead need to specify expected sensing usage. Objectives: We calibrate the information weight of a
𝜌-POMDP policy family to a sensing target and identify the role of Expected Free Energy (EFE) within that family. Methods:
We define an operational shadow price as a crossing threshold of the usage curve, distinguish it from a finite-grid bracket, and
construct an endpoint mixture for targets inside usage gaps. We prove scale equivariance of the receding-horizon planner and
stationary convergence of a projected controller under stated conditions. Minimizing the specified recursive, reward-based
EFE objective is equivalent to maximizing reward plus information gain at 𝑤 = 1 for observe-then-commit problems and
hidden-state-preserving actions in a factored extension. The reward–information identification is exact under log scoring and
otherwise depends on the declared reward convention. Results: Usage curves collapse after rescaling, and the controller
re-adapts empirically after a tenfold reward shift. Targets selected by a predeclared rule from calibration curves are met on
held-out seeds to within 0.13 observations. Calibration can nevertheless sacrifice reward relative to spending less or choosing
another mixture. Comparisons against usage-capped and target-matched references distinguish the cost of extra sensing from
shortfalls within the family. On real sensor records with learned likelihoods, two independently fixed targets transfer within
the represented collection period, one lies below the sampled range, and later batches defeat frozen calibration. Direct-penalty
and fixed-count policies achieve better classification accuracy. Conclusions: Usage calibration is appropriate when expected
sensing usage is the design requirement. It does not maximize reward under a cap. EFE supplies an information-unit anchor
under specified assumptions, while usage, reward, and success must be evaluated separately.

1    Introduction
An engineer designing an inspection policy may need to specify how much testing the system should perform
before deciding whether to repair or release an item. A standard partially observable Markov decision process
(POMDP) values a test through its effect on expected task reward. A 𝜌-POMDP additionally rewards properties
of the agent’s belief, allowing information gain to have explicit value (Araya-López et al. 2010). This introduces a
practical choice: how much weight should information gain receive relative to reward?
   We study a different design input, an expected sensing-usage target 𝐵. Such a target is useful when sensing is
itself a deliverable, as in a coverage, monitoring, or verification requirement. It specifies the average number of
tests or their expected cumulative cost. It is neither a hard limit on each episode nor an instruction to maximize
reward subject to spending at most 𝐵. Those alternatives define different design problems, and our experiments
measure the reward cost of meeting a target when reward alone would justify less sensing.
   For each information weight 𝑤, let 𝑈 (𝑤) be the expected number or cost of sensing actions under the resulting
policy. We seek a policy with usage 𝐵, but 𝑈 (𝑤) can jump over that target, stay flat, or cross it more than once.
We call its final crossing threshold the operational shadow price, 𝑤 ∗ (𝐵). This price is read from the usage curve.

48      Active inference identifies a particular point in this policy family. Its Expected Free Energy (EFE) objective
49   combines pragmatic value with expected information gain (K. Friston, Rigoli, et al. 2015; Parr and K. J. Friston 2019).
50   For the recursive, reward-based objective specified here, minimizing EFE is equivalent to maximizing reward plus
51   information gain at 𝑤 = 1. The reward–information identification is exact under log scoring (Bernardo 1979).
52   For ordinary task rewards it depends on the declared reward convention and the treatment of the preference
53   normalizer. The coefficient is therefore an untuned default under shared units, not a scale-invariant calibration.
54   In particular, 𝑤 = 1 belongs to the level set of its implicit budget 𝑈 (1), but need not equal that budget’s crossing
55   threshold.
56      The paper makes three contributions. First, it formulates expected-usage calibration within a 𝜌-POMDP
57   policy family, distinguishing the level set, crossing threshold, grid estimate, and endpoint mixture. It establishes
58   scale equivariance and a conditional stationary-convergence result for online control. Second, it proves the
59   EFE equivalence for observe-then-commit problems and extends it to the hidden-state-preserving actions of
60   factored observation POMDPs. A two-state analysis characterizes sensing onset and an interval of weights
61   that avoids an unnecessary second observation. Third, it evaluates calibration and the information-unit default
62   against planning baselines and offline references, then tests learned likelihoods on real sensor records with
63   independently fixed targets and a chronological transfer set. Code and experiment data are available at https:
64   //github.com/PatrickAllenCooper/rho_aif.
65      The evidence also sets limits on these contributions. Targets selected by a predeclared rule from calibration
66   curves are met on held-out seeds to within 0.13 observations, but the selected mixtures can sacrifice reward
67   relative to spending less or to the best sampled reference mixture meeting the same target, and need not be the
68   best mixtures in the weight family. The untuned 𝑤 = 1 lies in the reward-maximizing run of sampled weights
69   on three of five swept environments and is beaten on the other two. Its advantage over reward-only planning
70   depends on the observation structure and available planning depth, rather than on state-space size alone. Ordinary
71   information gain can spend the budget on uncertainty unrelated to reward, and the stated equivalence does not
72   cover observation actions that change the hidden state. These limitations remain part of the main argument, with
73   supporting controls and counterexamples in the appendices.
74      An abridged version was accepted at IWAI 2026 (Cooper and Velasquez 2026). It contains the EFE equiv-
75   alence results and their benchmark evaluation. The budgeted formulation, scale-equivariance and controller
76   results, budget experiments, and distractor and destructive-sensing analyses are extensions. We also revise the
77   workshop version’s claim that the advantage grows with state-space size and observation-action count, which
78   RockSample[11,11] does not support.

95    These are different uses of information within planning, and our comparisons do not establish superiority over
96    state-of-the-art POMDP solvers generally.
97       Araya-López et al. (2010) introduced 𝜌-POMDPs with belief-dependent rewards. Their 𝜌 denotes the whole
98    reward, whereas we reserve it for an information term added to the ordinary task reward. Convex belief rewards
99    support convex value functions and piecewise-linear convex approximations. Expected information gain is
100   concave in the belief, so that machinery does not apply directly. Fehr et al. (2018) treat non-convex rewards under
101   a Lipschitz hypothesis, and Benchetrit et al. (2025) develop an anytime planner for continuous spaces. For a finite
102   observation model, strictly positive likelihoods make expected information gain Lipschitz on the belief simplex.
103   Zero likelihoods, including noiseless sensing, can violate this condition. Our exact tree recursion does not rely on
104   that approximation guarantee.
105      POMDPs with information rewards were developed for active cooperative perception (Spaan et al. 2015), and
106   their relation to 𝜌-POMDPs is discussed by Satsangi et al. (2018). Earlier preference-elicitation work selected
107   queries by sequential value of information while retaining state-dependent rewards (Boutilier 2002). These
108   approaches establish the motivation for valuing information explicitly. Our focus is the coefficient on that value
109   and its relation to expected sensing usage.
110
111      Information value and experimental design. Classical value of information measures the improvement an
112   observation can bring to a decision (Howard 1966). Bayesian experimental design instead commonly selects
113   observations by expected information gain (Foster et al. 2021; Lindley 1956). The criteria coincide under a
114   particular reward model: when the reward is a proper log score, the expected improvement in score is the
115   expected information gain (Bernardo 1979). This supplies the exact reward–information identification used here.
116   Sequential experimental design is also the closest structural analogue to our observe-then-commit setting.
117      Dynamic feature acquisition can greedily select features by conditional mutual information, with learned
118   approximations to the required distributions (Covert et al. 2023). Our sensor-record study uses this established
119   greedy rule as a fixed-count comparator. Its purpose is to test usage calibration within an information-weighted
120   family, not introduce a new feature-selection principle.
121      Information-directed sampling (IDS) selects an action distribution to minimize the ratio of squared expected
122   one-period regret to expected information gain about the optimal action (Russo and Van Roy 2014). Walraven
123   et al. (2025) connect active inference with information gathering in POMDPs. Our contribution within this setting
124   is to define and evaluate a calibration procedure for the weight in a specified 𝜌-POMDP policy family.
125
126      Active inference and the EFE formulation. Active inference casts perception and action as variational inference (K.
127   Friston 2010; Parr, Pezzulo, et al. 2022). EFE combines pragmatic and epistemic terms (Da Costa, Parr, et al. 2020; K.
128   Friston, Rigoli, et al. 2015; Parr and K. J. Friston 2019). Its recursive sophisticated-inference form conditions future
129   choices on future beliefs (K. Friston, Da Costa, et al. 2021). Da Costa, Sajid, et al. (2023) prove reward-Bellman
130   optimality for finite-horizon recursive inference in the limit of precise reward-encoding preferences, with exact
131   state estimation. That limiting result does not imply that a fixed finite information weight is reward-optimal.
132      Several distinct constructions occur under the EFE name. Millidge, Tschantz, and Buckley (2021) distinguish
133   EFE from a naive future extension of variational free energy and propose Free Energy of the Expected Future
134   (FEEF). Champion et al. (2026) examine which EFE expressions follow from common root definitions and the
135   restrictions those roots impose on prior preferences. de Vries et al. (2025) derive EFE-based planning from a
136   model augmented with preference and epistemic priors. The generalised free energy of Parr and K. J. Friston
137   (2019) is another distinct functional. Our equivalence concerns only the recursive, reward-based objective defined
138   in Section 3, with expected information gain about the hidden state as its epistemic term. It does not assert an
139   unchanged coefficient for these alternative constructions. Broader comparisons with decision-theoretic objectives
140   include an EFE optimality-gap analysis (Wei 2024) and a survey of formal equivalences (Sweeney et al. 2026).

158      Budgets and dual control. Rational inattention constrains or prices information capacity (Matějka and McKay
159   2015; Sims 2003). Constrained MDPs use Lagrange multipliers on expected resource costs (Altman 1999), and
160   constrained POMDP methods track admissible cost alongside belief (Kim et al. 2011). Automatic temperature
161   tuning in soft actor-critic drives policy entropy toward a target (Haarnoja, Zhou, Hartikainen, et al. 2018). These
162   provide close conceptual precedents for resource control. Our weight multiplies information gain, however,
163   while the target concerns observation count or cost. It is not generally the multiplier of a sensing-cap constraint.
164   Section 4.6 gives the detailed distinctions, including the difference from a dual formulation of EFE itself (Kouw
165   2026). The contribution is an operational calibration for this policy family, its exact scale transformation under
166   stated assumptions, and explicit treatment of finite-grid crossings and usage gaps.

      4.6       Positioning against Prior Art
587   Information prices and resource constraints are classical in constrained MDPs (Altman 1999), rational inattention
588   (Matějka and McKay 2015; Sims 2003), and SAC (Haarnoja, Zhou, Hartikainen, et al. 2018). Here one information
589   weight controls sensing usage across episodes. It differs from an information constraint internal to EFE (Kouw
590   2026) and per-node constrained-POMDP dual ascent (Stocco et al. 2024). The contribution is the calibration
591   procedure for this policy family, with count/cost equivariance, grid crossings, endpoint mixtures and conditional
592   stationary convergence.
593      A constrained-POMDP solver may randomize belief-dependently and outperform a mixture that fixes one
594   weight for each episode (Kim et al. 2011). Our references use sampled SARSOP policies and estimated feasibility,
595   with approximation, sampling and selection uncertainty. The shallow RockSample reference does not reach EFE’s
596   operating region (Appendix H.7).
597      Offline calibration requires a weight sweep. Its benefit is a requirement stated in sensing units, rather than
598   reduced sampling. Under its hypotheses, the online controller can find a crossing without a sweep. Scale equiv-
599   ariance also transfers directly tuned weights on a correspondingly rescaled grid, so that benefit is not unique to
600   usage calibration.

603   We evaluate two claims separately. The budget experiments ask whether an information-weighted policy family

      maximizing E[𝑅] − 𝜆E[𝑈 ] over the POMDP policy space, with 𝛾=0.999 and five seeds of 300 episodes per penalty.

      The penalty is the Lagrangian price of the usage cap in Equation 6. The information weight 𝑤 is not that multiplier.

         At budget 𝐵, the reference is the feasible envelope of the sampled policy points, obtained by maximizing their
872
      mixture reward subject to mixture usage at most 𝐵. A solution uses at most two sampled policies. Policies below
873
      the target remain eligible, and the reference is flat at the largest sampled reward once the cap is slack. This is
874
      a linear program over measured points, not equal-usage interpolation. The solver is approximate, the penalty
875
      grid is finite, and reward and usage are Monte Carlo estimates. Consequently the envelope is neither a certified
876
      population bound nor the full constrained frontier. We do not assume that finite-state strong-duality results
877
      (Altman 1999) automatically extend to the continuous belief MDP. Appendix J.2 identifies the evaluated policies

      and reference records.

       8   Discussion
1393      Choosing a sensing requirement. Expected usage is an interpretable design input when sensing itself is required.
1394   It controls neither reward efficiency nor relevance. The endpoint mixture can meet a target while earning less
1395   than another mixture at that usage, or than a policy spending less. Ordinary information gain can also pay
1396   for irrelevant uncertainty. Reward-equivalence scoring removes distractor spending in the tested factorized
1397   benchmark, but does not prevent over-observation (Section 6.10). The simulated targets are calibration-derived.
1398   The sensor-record targets were fixed independently of usage curves, but are researcher-defined rather than
1399   application requirements. Their later-batch failures require a fresh transfer check before using a frozen calibration.
1400
          Interpreting the information-unit default. The recursive EFE identity fixes 𝑤 = 1 after reward and information
1401
       units are declared. That weight belongs to the level set of 𝑈 (1), but need not equal its crossing threshold.
1402
       Its usefulness is empirical: it lies in the reward-maximizing run of sampled weights on three of five swept
1403
       environments and is beaten on two. Larger weights can buy greater success at a reward cost. Reward, success
1404
       and usage therefore require separate evaluation.
1405
1406      Observation structure and planning depth. The advantage depends on sensing opportunities that the search can
1407   represent. State-space size and sensor count alone do not predict it. The largest Tileworld separation is against a
1408   fixed-horizon policy that takes no scans. On RockSample[11,11] the weighted family gains nothing over Planning,
1409   and the standalone heuristic beats the tree-search agents on the two larger instances. Navigation supplies another
1410
                                                                                                 Manuscript prepared for JAIR review.
       32   •   Cooper and Velasquez


1411   negative case, with no epistemic agent significantly beating the greedy mover at any tested size (Appendix D.1).
1412   These results do not imply superiority over reward-only planning at arbitrary depth.
1413      At one-step depth, the recursion is myopic reward plus information gain at 𝑤 = 1. Horizon, discounting and
1414   model accuracy affect its behavior. The finite-horizon comparisons above and the controls in Appendices D.2
1415   and D.3 show these dependencies. The observed discount threshold is not a general regime boundary. Every
1416   tested Tiger sensor mismatch affects EFE and Planning identically, providing no EFE-specific robustness benefit.
1417   Overestimated sensor accuracy can cause premature commitment.
1418
1419
          What the search controls establish. MCTS-EFE addresses the cost of exact belief-tree enumeration. Its main
1420
       POMCP comparison matches simulation counts with different costs per simulation, and a separate Tiger control
1421
       matches computation. Tuning both solvers on disjoint seeds leaves MCTS-EFE ahead on Tiger reward and
1422
       success. The Diagnosis and Tileworld gaps also survive the tested exploration constants and information-gain
1423
       rollouts. However, neither in-tree information gain nor max-backup has a detectable success or reward effect
1424
       after multiplicity correction at five seeds. EFE leaf evaluation, exact beliefs and exact commit-child values remain
1425
       coupled, so the experiments do not isolate directed observation selection or establish general solver superiority
1426
       (Appendices L and F.2).
1427      Structural and practical limits. The reduction requires hidden-state-preserving observation and navigation
1428   actions. State-changing sensing needs transition-aware predictive and posterior beliefs. The diagnostic Δ𝑇 is
1429   neither a proved additive correction nor a decision-error bound, and perfect sensing can make it infinite after
1430   arbitrarily small drift (Example H.2, Appendix H). The simulators use known models, and Structural Inspection
1431   uses a synthetic task and a hand-coded leaf rule. The sensor-record study learns a model, but its shallow planner
1432   and conditional-independence approximation establish no general acquisition advantage. More efficient search,
1433   transition-aware budget control and validation against an operational sensing requirement remain open.
1434
1435   9    Conclusion
1436
       An expected sensing-usage requirement can calibrate the information-gain weight of a 𝜌-POMDP policy family.
1437
       The operational shadow price is a crossing threshold, estimated by a grid bracket. Under stated conditions, mixing
1438
       the bracket’s endpoint policies meets a usage-gap target in expectation. The receding-horizon planner is scale
1439
       equivariant, and a projected decaying-step controller has a stationary-convergence guarantee under explicit
1440
       hypotheses. Re-adaptation after reward-scale shifts is an empirical result, including a useful reset heuristic that
1441
       the theorem does not cover.
1442
          The simulated held-out study shows accurate usage calibration but also its reward cost. Real sensor records
1443
       extend the test to learned likelihoods and targets fixed independently of usage curves. Two targets transfer within
1444
       the represented collection period, one lies below the sampled range, and later batches defeat frozen calibration.
1445
       Direct cost tuning and fixed-count information acquisition also give better test accuracy in that configuration.
1446
       Calibration is therefore a way to adapt an already-selected information-weighted family to a feasible expected-use
1447
       target, subject to a transfer check. It is not a reason to prefer that family when directly counting or penalizing
1448
       accesses meets the design need.
1449
          The recursive EFE objective supplies an information-unit anchor at 𝑤 = 1, with exact reward–information
1450
       identification under log scoring and an explicit convention for ordinary rewards. This weight is competitive in
1451
       several benchmarks and suboptimal in others. Its practical value depends on the observation structure, planning
1452
       depth, and model assumptions. The resulting design procedure is to state the intended usage, calibrate within a
1453
       specified policy family, and assess usage, reward, and success together.

       F.2   Observe-then-commit POMCP and MCTS-EFE
1744
1745   The observe-then-commit POMCP adaptation (Silver and Veness 2010) has two fidelity gaps absent from the
1746   separate RockSample implementation. The depth-cap leaf uses the best commit reward for the sampled state, an
1747   optimistic clairvoyant value. Rollout commits instead receive their belief-expected reward, replacing sampled
1748   payoff with conditional expectation. Their separate policy effects are not isolated. Rollouts start from the simulated

       J.4   Distractor Comparison Implementation
2145
       The IDS control in Section 6.10 minimizes Δ2 /𝐼 over individual observation actions. Here Δ is the opportunity
2146
       cost of observing rather than committing and 𝐼 measures information about the optimal commit. When that
2147
       information vanishes, the adaptation substitutes state-entropy gain. Information about the optimal commit is
2148
       correctly zero for the distractor, but the fallback rewards learning its nuisance bit. This deterministic action
2149
       choice does not implement IDS over randomized action distributions. The observed distractor spending therefore
2150
       identifies a failure of this adaptation, not of reward-aware information measures generally.

2190   Exact enumeration costs O ((𝐾 |O|) 𝐻 ) for 𝐾 sensing actions with |O| outcomes each. MCTS-EFE uses UCB1 over
2191   observe/commit actions, enumerates each observation action’s outcomes, adds exact per-node information gain,

2193   observation. Commit-child values are exact belief-expected rewards. The default exploration constant is the fixed
2194   commit-reward range, 110 on Tiger, rather than the preliminary-return range used by Silver and Veness (2010).
2195   Appendix F.2 reports exploration tuning, informed rollouts, the ablation and a separate compute-matched Tiger
2196   control.

2198   percentage points against POMCP’s 88.1% ± 0.2 (𝑝 = 4.4 × 10−6 ). Its cost is 14–16 ms per episode across tested
2199   horizons, versus 11–12 ms for POMCP, so matching simulations does not match computation. At 𝐻 = 5 and 200
2200   simulations on Diagnosis, the corresponding success rates are 94.2% ± 0.5 and 65.5% ± 2.1 (𝑝 = 1.1 × 10−4 ). These
2201   are seed-level SEs and Welch tests from results_mcts_efe.csv.
