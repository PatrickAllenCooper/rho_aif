# Independent evidence and significance review

**Recommendation: ACCEPT.** This is an evidence/reproducibility and contribution-scope judgment on the artifacts identified in `independent_evidence_final_snapshot.json`. No required scientific correction remains in this review's scope. The recommendation does not inherit or count earlier agents' accept decisions, and does not predict the journal's editorial decision.

The publishable contribution is a careful account of a specified information-weighted policy family: what the restricted EFE correspondence means, how expected-use calibration works, and why matching usage does not imply efficient decisions or transfer. The new study makes that account more informative by fitting likelihoods on public recorded observations, fixing targets independently of measured usage curves, and retaining a failed target and adverse comparisons. It does not establish a superior acquisition algorithm or an operational benefit. The final abstract, main section, appendix, discussion, and conclusion make these distinctions explicit. That bounded contribution is sufficient for my acceptance recommendation when considered with the paper's theoretical characterization and controlled simulation evidence.

The result materially changes the practical reading of the method. On the within-period test, the B=4 and B=8 crossings use 4.0056265 and 8.0188874 sensors, while B=2 is below the smallest usage among sampled weights. The two available targets satisfy their predeclared conditional interval criterion; the all-three-target criterion fails. Crossing accuracy is 80.2266% and 76.2786%, versus 82.1147% and 80.1847% for direct equality mixtures. Those direct mixtures use 3.96536 and 7.97691 sensors, so the comparison is close in observed usage but not an exact held-out equality. Exact-count CMI also has higher accuracy. All four nominal paired primary accuracy-gap intervals exclude zero on the unfavorable side. The later batches overshoot both targets, by approximately 1.15–2.51 accesses at B=4 and 1.49–4.73 at B=8. The study supports the manuscript's qualified calibration claim and its adverse transfer finding, without an application-success narrative.

**Reporting and limitations.** The main section retains every target, names the sampled-grid scope of infeasibility, reports the direct comparator's realized usage, and makes the classification shortfalls and temporal overspending visible. The uncertainty correctly includes calibration reselection for the within-period contrasts and holds the original selections fixed for later batches. It conditions on one fitted model and exchangeability within batch/class strata. It does not include retraining, unknown laboratory-trial dependence, or variation across instruments or sites. Nominal pointwise H2 intervals are not presented as a family-wide superiority result. The manuscript also reports poor full-record log loss, avoiding treatment of the all-sensor classifier as an oracle.

The UCI primary record independently confirms the sensor-block ordering, record count, classes, chronology, and corrected batch 10. PMLR confirms the established greedy-CMI lineage; the study does not claim to reproduce that learned method. My acceptance recommendation rests on the accuracy, auditability, and explanatory value of the bounded result.
