\title{Variational Free Energy as a Generalized Utility Function for $\rho$-POMDPs: Principled Epistemic Foraging via Active Inference}
\author{Patrick Cooper \and David Baines}
\date{February 25, 2026}

\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether variational free energy (VFE), cast as the generalized utility function $\rho$ in $\rho$-POMDPs, produces superior epistemic foraging behavior compared to standard reward-maximizing POMDP policies and alternative belief-state utility functions. By substituting Expected Free Energy (EFE) from the active inference literature as $\rho$, we obtain an agent whose exploration--exploitation balance arises from a single, principled objective rather than hand-tuned information bonuses. Across two discrete POMDP environments---the Tiger problem and a two-state information-seeking testbed---the VFE agent achieves the highest success rates (99.1\% and 96.4\%, respectively) and is the only agent to achieve positive mean reward on the Tiger problem ($+4.70$ vs.\ $-5.85$ for the myopic baseline). Critically, the VFE agent exhibits this behavior without per-environment parameter tuning, while the information-gain agent's hand-tuned weight proves fragile across reward scales.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Decision-making under partial observability requires agents to balance two competing demands: exploiting current knowledge to maximize reward, and gathering information to reduce uncertainty about the hidden state of the world. In standard POMDPs, information gathering has no intrinsic value; it is only instrumentally useful insofar as it leads to higher expected reward. This creates a well-known difficulty: the exploration--exploitation trade-off must be resolved either by the planning horizon (which is often intractable) or by heuristic exploration bonuses.

The $\rho$-POMDP framework \cite{araya2010} addresses this by extending POMDPs with a belief-dependent utility function $\rho(b)$ that allows the agent to derive value directly from properties of its belief state. This enables explicit optimization over uncertainty reduction, information gain, or other belief-state properties alongside task reward.

Separately, the Active Inference (AIF) framework \cite{friston2010,parr2019} offers a unified account of perception and action as processes that minimize variational free energy. Under AIF, an agent selects policies that minimize Expected Free Energy (EFE), which naturally decomposes into a \emph{pragmatic} term (goal-seeking) and an \emph{epistemic} term (information-seeking). Crucially, this decomposition is not hand-tuned: both terms arise from the same objective function.

We propose a formal bridge between these frameworks by substituting EFE as the $\rho$ function in $\rho$-POMDPs. This yields an agent whose epistemic foraging behavior is a natural consequence of its objective, rather than an engineered exploration bonus. We test whether this principled integration produces measurably different---and superior---epistemic behavior compared to standard approaches.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{The $\rho$-POMDP Framework}

A $\rho$-POMDP extends the standard POMDP tuple $\langle S, A, O, T, \Omega, R, \gamma \rangle$ with a belief-dependent utility function $\rho : \Delta(S) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, where $\Delta(S)$ is the belief simplex over states. The agent's objective becomes:

\begin{equation}
    \pi^* = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{H} \gamma^t \left( R(s_t, a_t) + \rho(b_t) \right) \right]
\end{equation}

The choice of $\rho$ determines the agent's epistemic character. When $\rho = 0$, we recover the standard POMDP. When $\rho$ encodes information gain, the agent is explicitly rewarded for reducing uncertainty.

\subsection{Expected Free Energy as $\rho$}

We define $\rho$ via the Expected Free Energy (EFE) from active inference. For a policy $\pi$ at future time $\tau$, the EFE is:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}(\pi) = \underbrace{- \mathbb{E}_{Q(o_\tau|\pi)}[\ln P(o_\tau|C)]}_{\text{Pragmatic Value}} - \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{Q(o_\tau|\pi)}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[Q(s_\tau|o_\tau, \pi) \,\|\, Q(s_\tau|\pi)\right]\right]}_{\text{Epistemic Value}}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $Q(s_\tau|\pi)$ is the prior predictive belief over states given policy $\pi$
    \item $Q(s_\tau|o_\tau, \pi)$ is the posterior belief after observing $o_\tau$
    \item $P(o_\tau|C)$ is the preferred outcome distribution encoding the agent's goals
    \item $D_{\mathrm{KL}}[\cdot \| \cdot]$ is the Kullback--Leibler divergence, measuring expected information gain
\end{itemize}

The pragmatic term drives the agent toward goal-consistent observations. The epistemic term drives the agent toward actions that maximally resolve uncertainty about hidden states. Both terms emerge from the same variational objective; there are no tunable weights balancing exploration against exploitation.

For discrete state-spaces, we compute EFE exactly via Bayesian belief updates over the generative model, following the standard approach in the AIF literature \cite{dacosta2020}.

\subsection{Agent Specifications}

We compare three agents that differ only in their objective function:

\paragraph{Myopic Reward-Maximizing Agent.} Selects actions via one-step lookahead over expected reward. Equivalent to a $\rho$-POMDP with $\rho = 0$. Serves as the baseline.

\paragraph{Information Gain Agent.} A $\rho$-POMDP agent with $\rho(b) = H(b) - \mathbb{E}[H(b')]$, i.e., expected entropy reduction. Represents the standard approach to belief-dependent utility in the $\rho$-POMDP literature.

\paragraph{VFE Agent.} Selects actions by minimizing EFE (Equation 2) over a recursive planning horizon. At each decision point, the agent evaluates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}(\text{observe}) = c_{\text{obs}} - I(b) + \mathbb{E}_{o}\!\left[\min_a \mathcal{G}(a \mid b'_o)\right]
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{obs}}$ is the observation cost, $I(b) = H(b) - \mathbb{E}_o[H(b'_o)]$ is the expected information gain at the current belief, and the expectation recurses over posterior beliefs $b'_o$ up to a planning horizon $H$. For commit actions, $\mathcal{G}(\text{commit}_i) = -\mathbb{E}_b[R_i]$, the negative expected reward under the current belief. The agent selects $\arg\min_a \mathcal{G}(a)$. No explicit information-seeking bonus is required; epistemic foraging emerges from the intrinsic information gain term $I(b)$ combined with the recursive evaluation of improved future decisions.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Tiger Problem}

The Tiger problem \cite{kaelbling1998} is a canonical POMDP benchmark that isolates epistemic foraging behavior. An agent faces two doors, behind one of which is a tiger (penalty) and behind the other a reward. The agent can listen (receiving a noisy observation about the tiger's location) or open a door (terminating the episode).

\begin{itemize}
    \item States: $\{$tiger\_left, tiger\_right$\}$
    \item Actions: $\{$listen, open\_left, open\_right$\}$
    \item Observations: $\{$hear\_left, hear\_right$\}$
    \item Listen accuracy: 0.85
    \item Rewards: correct door $+10$, wrong door $-100$, listen cost $-1$
\end{itemize}

The extreme reward asymmetry ($+10$ vs.\ $-100$) makes information gathering critical: committing prematurely is catastrophic. This amplifies behavioral differences between agents with different epistemic strategies.

\subsection{Two-State Information-Seeking Testbed}

A minimal environment with two hidden states, noisy observations (75\% accuracy), and symmetric reward structure (correct $+1$, incorrect $-1$, observation cost $-0.1$). Serves as a simpler validation testbed.

Both environments are implemented as OpenAI Gymnasium environments for standardization and reproducibility.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Belief Convergence Rate}: Steps to reach a belief confidence threshold; belief entropy reduction curve.
    \item \textbf{Sample Efficiency}: Number of observations required before committing; variance across trials.
    \item \textbf{Policy Quality}: Expected cumulative reward; success rate; statistical significance via t-tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

All experiments were run for 1{,}000 episodes per agent. Statistical significance was assessed via independent two-sample $t$-tests.

\end{tabular}
\end{table}

The VFE agent is the only agent to achieve positive mean reward on the Tiger problem. The Myopic and Information Gain agents both listen exactly once before committing, yielding success rates near the single-observation accuracy of 85\% and large negative mean rewards driven by catastrophic losses on incorrect commitments. The VFE agent listens $4.31$ times on average, reaching belief confidence of $0.995$ before committing. Its reward variance ($\pm 10.55$) is roughly one quarter that of the other agents ($\pm 37$--$41$), reflecting far more consistent outcomes.

The Information Gain agent performs no better than Myopic on this environment despite its explicit exploration bonus ($p = 0.06$, n.s.\ for reward). The information gain weight of $1.0$---appropriate for the two-state testbed---is insufficient relative to Tiger's reward scale, demonstrating the fragility of hand-tuned epistemic parameters.

All comparisons between VFE and both baselines are statistically significant at $p < 0.001$ for both observation count and reward.

The VFE agent achieves the highest success rate ($96.4\%$) and lowest final belief entropy ($0.222$ bits), indicating the strongest belief convergence at decision time. However, the Information Gain agent achieves the highest mean reward ($+0.51$ vs.\ $+0.38$, $p < 0.001$), because its more moderate exploration (3.24 observations vs.\ 5.51) avoids accumulating excess observation costs in an environment where the penalty for premature commitment is mild.

This result is informative: in low-stakes environments, the VFE agent's constitutive epistemic drive produces ``over-exploration'' relative to the reward-optimal strategy. The agent gathers more information than is instrumentally justified because uncertainty reduction has intrinsic value under EFE. Whether this is a strength or a weakness depends on the application: in safety-critical domains (cf.\ the Tiger problem), this epistemic conservatism is precisely what prevents catastrophic outcomes.

\subsection{Cross-Environment Robustness}

A critical finding is that the VFE agent operates identically across both environments without any parameter adjustment. Its planning horizon ($H = 4$ for the testbed, $H = 6$ for Tiger) sets the depth of recursive evaluation, but no epistemic weight is tuned per-environment. In contrast, the Information Gain agent's performance is sensitive to its \texttt{info\_gain\_weight}: a weight of $1.0$ is effective on the testbed but insufficient for the Tiger problem's reward scale. This exemplifies the advantage of deriving exploration from a principled objective rather than an additive bonus.

\section{Hypotheses}

\paragraph{H1 (Tractability) --- Supported.} VFE serves as an effective generalized utility function for $\rho$-POMDPs: the formal substitution is coherent, the resulting framework remains tractable for discrete state-spaces via recursive Bayesian evaluation, and the induced policies are well-defined. Computational cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(|\mathcal{O}|^H)$ in the planning horizon $H$ and observation cardinality $|\mathcal{O}|$, which is manageable for the small discrete environments studied here.

\paragraph{H2 (Epistemic Superiority) --- Partially Supported.} The VFE agent achieves the highest success rates on both environments (99.1\% on Tiger, 96.4\% on the testbed) and the strongest belief convergence at decision time (confidence $\geq 0.96$). On the Tiger problem, it is the only agent with positive mean reward. However, on the low-stakes testbed, the VFE agent's additional exploration reduces net reward relative to the Information Gain agent ($+0.38$ vs.\ $+0.51$). Epistemic superiority manifests most clearly when the cost of premature commitment is high relative to the cost of observation.

\paragraph{H3 (Constitutive Epistemics) --- Supported.} The VFE agent exhibits qualitatively different epistemic behavior. Its ``over-exploration'' on the testbed---gathering information beyond what is instrumentally optimal---reflects a constitutive drive to reduce uncertainty, not merely an instrumental one. This behavioral signature has direct implications for alignment: agents whose utility is defined over beliefs are predictably cautious, with behavior that degrades gracefully rather than catastrophically when faced with uncertainty. The Tiger results demonstrate this concretely: the VFE agent's reward variance ($\pm 10.55$) is one quarter that of baselines ($\pm 37$--$41$), indicating far more consistent and interpretable behavior under partial observability.

\section{Intrinsic Exploration--Exploitation Balancing}

A key theoretical claim of this framework is that the exploration--exploitation trade-off resolves itself dynamically. When the belief state $Q(s)$ has high entropy (high uncertainty), the information gain term $I(b)$ in Equation 3 is large, reducing $\mathcal{G}(\text{observe})$ and favoring information-seeking actions. As observations accumulate and beliefs sharpen, $I(b)$ decays toward zero and the pragmatic commit value dominates, shifting behavior toward goal realization. This transition requires no scheduling, annealing, or hyperparameter adjustment.

Our results confirm this mechanism empirically. On the Tiger problem, the VFE agent listens an average of 4.31 times---accumulating evidence until belief confidence reaches 0.995---before committing. The resulting 99.1\% success rate and positive mean reward ($+4.70$) demonstrate that the dynamic balance settles at the right point for this high-stakes environment. On the lower-stakes testbed, the same mechanism produces more exploration (5.51 observations, confidence 0.964) than is reward-optimal, but still achieves 96.4\% success. The agent adapts its exploration depth to the environment's structure without any explicit instruction to do so: the reward asymmetry of the Tiger problem naturally inflates $\mathcal{G}(\text{commit})$ at uncertain beliefs, extending the exploration phase until sufficient evidence has been gathered.

\section{Future Work}

The discrete state-space formulation presented here establishes the theoretical and empirical foundation. Natural extensions include:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Scaled inference}: Variational message passing and amortized inference for continuous or high-dimensional state spaces.
    \item \textbf{LLM integration}: Using large language models as generative world models to parameterize the transition dynamics $P(s'|s,a)$ and observation likelihoods $P(o|s)$, enabling EFE computation in semantic state spaces via counterfactual simulation.
    \item \textbf{Extended benchmarks}: Multi-armed bandits with hidden structure, partially observable navigation, and sequential diagnosis problems.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical models}: Nested generative models with beliefs at multiple levels of abstraction.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Novelty Statement}

The novelty of this work lies in formally bridging the $\rho$-POMDP and active inference frameworks by substituting Expected Free Energy as the belief-dependent utility function $\rho$. While $\rho$-POMDPs provide the formal mechanism for belief-dependent optimization and active inference provides the principled objective, neither literature has systematically explored this integration. Our contribution is both theoretical---showing the substitution is coherent and tractable---and empirical: the VFE agent achieves 99.1\% success on the Tiger problem (the only agent with positive mean reward) and 96.4\% on an information-seeking testbed, without per-environment tuning of exploration parameters. These results demonstrate that principled epistemic foraging via EFE is not merely theoretically elegant but practically superior in environments where the cost of premature commitment is high.
