\begin{abstract}
We investigate whether variational free energy (VFE), cast as the generalized utility function $\rho$ in $\rho$-POMDPs, produces superior epistemic foraging behavior compared to same-horizon reward-only planning, per-environment tuned information gain, and pymdp's reference active inference agent. By substituting Expected Free Energy (EFE) from the active inference literature as $\rho$, we obtain an agent whose exploration--exploitation balance arises from a single, principled objective rather than hand-tuned information bonuses. Across five discrete POMDP environments with six baselines, we find that VFE's advantage is \emph{environment-dependent}: on simple observe-then-commit problems (Tiger), same-horizon reward-only planning matches VFE's 99\% success rate; on multi-observation-action environments requiring \emph{which}-information-to-gather decisions (Diagnosis, Bandit), VFE significantly outperforms same-horizon planning ($+9.1$ pp and $+20.6$ pp respectively, $p < 0.05$). Per-environment tuned information gain achieves high success rates but requires weights varying from $w=20$ to $w=100$ across environments and systematically over-explores relative to VFE. VFE achieves competitive or superior performance across all environments with zero per-environment tuning, identifying structured uncertainty as the regime where principled epistemic foraging is most valuable.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Decision-making under partial observability requires agents to balance two competing demands: exploiting current knowledge to maximize reward, and gathering information to reduce uncertainty about the hidden state of the world. In standard POMDPs, information gathering has no intrinsic value; it is only instrumentally useful insofar as it leads to higher expected reward. This creates a well-known difficulty: the exploration--exploitation trade-off must be resolved either by the planning horizon (which is often intractable) or by heuristic exploration bonuses.

The $\rho$-POMDP framework \cite{araya2010} addresses this by extending POMDPs with a belief-dependent utility function $\rho(b)$ that allows the agent to derive value directly from properties of its belief state. This enables explicit optimization over uncertainty reduction, information gain, or other belief-state properties alongside task reward.

Separately, the Active Inference (AIF) framework \cite{friston2010,parr2019} offers a unified account of perception and action as processes that minimize variational free energy. Under AIF, an agent selects policies that minimize Expected Free Energy (EFE), which naturally decomposes into a \emph{pragmatic} term (goal-seeking) and an \emph{epistemic} term (information-seeking). Crucially, this decomposition is not hand-tuned: both terms arise from the same objective function.

We propose a formal bridge between these frameworks by substituting EFE as the $\rho$ function in $\rho$-POMDPs. This yields an agent whose epistemic foraging behavior is a natural consequence of its objective, rather than an engineered exploration bonus. We test whether this principled integration produces measurably different---and superior---epistemic behavior compared to standard approaches.

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{POMDPs and Belief-Space Planning}

Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes provide the standard formalism for sequential decision-making under state uncertainty \cite{kaelbling1998}. Exact solutions require planning over the continuous belief simplex, which is computationally intractable for all but the smallest problems. Point-based value iteration methods---including PBVI, Perseus, and HSVI---address this by restricting computation to a finite subset of reachable beliefs, enabling scalability to problems with thousands of states \cite{shani2013}. The Tiger problem, introduced by Kaelbling et al.\ \cite{kaelbling1998}, remains a canonical benchmark for evaluating POMDP algorithms because its extreme reward asymmetry isolates the role of information gathering in decision quality.

A persistent limitation of standard POMDPs is that information gathering has no intrinsic value: the agent can only justify exploration instrumentally, through its eventual contribution to reward. This makes the exploration--exploitation trade-off dependent on the planning horizon, and myopic or short-horizon solvers systematically under-explore.

\subsection{$\rho$-POMDPs and Belief-Dependent Rewards}

Araya et al.\ \cite{araya2010} introduced the $\rho$-POMDP framework to address this limitation by augmenting the reward function with a belief-dependent utility $\rho(b)$. When $\rho$ is convex, the resulting value function retains convexity, preserving compatibility with standard POMDP solvers via piecewise linear and convex (PWLC) approximations. Common choices for $\rho$ include entropy reduction, KL divergence from a target belief, and information gain, each encoding a different notion of ``epistemic value.''

Recent work has extended $\rho$-POMDPs to continuous state and observation spaces. Lim et al.\ \cite{lim2023} developed $\rho$-POMCPOW, an anytime online solver that dynamically refines belief representations for continuous $\rho$-POMDPs, enabling applications such as sensor placement and active localization. These extensions demonstrate the practical relevance of belief-dependent rewards but leave open the question of \emph{which} $\rho$ to use---a choice that remains largely heuristic and environment-specific.

\subsection{Active Inference and Expected Free Energy}

The Active Inference framework, rooted in the Free Energy Principle \cite{friston2010}, casts perception and action as dual processes of approximate Bayesian inference. An agent maintains a generative model of its environment and selects policies that minimize Expected Free Energy (EFE), which decomposes into pragmatic value (alignment with preferred outcomes) and epistemic value (expected information gain) \cite{parr2019}. Da Costa et al.\ \cite{dacosta2020} provided a comprehensive synthesis of active inference on discrete state-spaces, deriving the mathematical framework from first principles and relating it to neuronal dynamics.

A key development is \emph{sophisticated inference} \cite{friston2021}, which extends active inference with recursive, tree-search-based planning over belief trajectories. Da Costa et al.\ \cite{dacosta2020b} showed that while standard active inference produces Bellman-optimal actions only for single-step horizons, the recursive sophisticated scheme achieves Bellman optimality for any finite temporal horizon on POMDPs. Our VFE agent's recursive EFE evaluation (Equation 3) is closely related to this sophisticated inference framework, adapted to the $\rho$-POMDP setting.

The \texttt{pymdp} library \cite{heins2022} provides a reference implementation of active inference for discrete state-spaces, offering modular components for inference, control, and learning. Our implementation draws on the same mathematical foundations but embeds them within the $\rho$-POMDP architecture to enable direct comparison with non-AIF agents.

\subsection{Exploration in Reinforcement Learning}

The exploration--exploitation trade-off has generated a large literature in reinforcement learning, with approaches spanning several distinct paradigms.

\paragraph{Bayesian approaches.} Bayes-Adaptive MDPs (BAMDPs) \cite{duff2002} augment the state space with an information state representing the agent's posterior over environment parameters, enabling Bayes-optimal exploration. While theoretically elegant, exact BAMDP solutions are intractable; approximations include Monte-Carlo tree search \cite{guez2013} and variational methods. BAMDPs address model uncertainty (``which MDP am I in?'') rather than state uncertainty (``which state am I in?''), making them complementary to---rather than competitive with---the $\rho$-POMDP/AIF approach studied here.

\paragraph{Intrinsic motivation and curiosity.} Schmidhuber \cite{schmidhuber1991} and Oudeyer et al.\ \cite{oudeyer2007} pioneered the idea that agents should be intrinsically motivated to seek novel or surprising states. Pathak et al.\ \cite{pathak2017} operationalized this as prediction error in a learned feature space, enabling curiosity-driven exploration in high-dimensional environments with sparse rewards. Bellemare et al.\ \cite{bellemare2016} unified count-based exploration and intrinsic motivation through density-model-derived pseudo-counts, showing that both approaches are grounded in information gain.

\paragraph{Information-theoretic methods.} VIME \cite{houthooft2016} uses variational inference in Bayesian neural networks to compute information gain about environment dynamics, rewarding actions that maximally update the agent's model. This approach is conceptually close to the epistemic term in EFE, but is applied to model learning (reducing parametric uncertainty) rather than state inference (reducing belief-state uncertainty), and requires an additive bonus weight to balance against task reward.

A common thread across these approaches is that exploration is engineered as a bonus or auxiliary objective, requiring tuning to balance against exploitation. Our work differs by deriving exploration from EFE, where pragmatic and epistemic values emerge from the same objective without a tunable weighting parameter.

\subsection{Bridging Active Inference and Reinforcement Learning}

Several recent works have established formal connections between active inference and reinforcement learning. The most comprehensive is Sajid et al.\ \cite{sajid2021}, who provide an accessible tutorial of discrete-state active inference alongside a direct empirical comparison with Q-learning and Bayesian model-based RL on the FrozenLake Gym environment. Their central finding is that behaviors typically engineered into RL agents---directed exploration, uncertainty-sensitive decision-making, and reward-free learning---arise naturally in active inference from the EFE objective alone. They further show that AIF agents can learn prior preferences from interaction, eliminating the need for hand-crafted reward functions. Millidge et al.\ \cite{millidge2020} formalized the relationship between active inference and control-as-inference, showing that the primary difference lies in how value is incorporated into the generative model. Da Costa et al.\ \cite{dacosta2020b} showed that sophisticated active inference---a recursive form of EFE evaluation---recovers Bellman-optimal policies for any finite temporal horizon on POMDPs.

Our work is complementary to, but distinct from, this line of research. Sajid et al.\ ask whether AIF is a viable \emph{alternative} to RL; we take AIF's viability as established and ask a different question: what happens when EFE is used as the belief-dependent utility function $\rho$ within the $\rho$-POMDP formalism? This reframing has several consequences. First, it enables controlled comparison of different $\rho$ functions (myopic, information gain, EFE) within the same decision architecture, isolating the effect of the utility function rather than confounding it with architectural differences between AIF and RL. Second, it inherits the formal properties of $\rho$-POMDPs (e.g., convexity conditions, compatibility with POMDP solvers) while retaining AIF's principled exploration. Third, it enables our analysis of cross-environment robustness and constitutive epistemics (RQ3), which addresses the \emph{character} of the resulting agency rather than its framework of origin.

\section{Methodology}

\subsection{The $\rho$-POMDP Framework}

A $\rho$-POMDP extends the standard POMDP tuple $\langle S, A, O, T, \Omega, R, \gamma \rangle$ with a belief-dependent utility function $\rho : \Delta(S) \rightarrow \mathbb{R}$, where $\Delta(S)$ is the belief simplex over states. The agent's objective becomes:

\begin{equation}
    \pi^* = \arg\max_\pi \mathbb{E}_\pi \left[ \sum_{t=0}^{H} \gamma^t \left( R(s_t, a_t) + \rho(b_t) \right) \right]
\end{equation}

The choice of $\rho$ determines the agent's epistemic character. When $\rho = 0$, we recover the standard POMDP. When $\rho$ encodes information gain, the agent is explicitly rewarded for reducing uncertainty.

\subsection{Expected Free Energy as $\rho$}

We define $\rho$ via the Expected Free Energy (EFE) from active inference. For a policy $\pi$ at future time $\tau$, the EFE is:

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}(\pi) = \underbrace{- \mathbb{E}_{Q(o_\tau|\pi)}[\ln P(o_\tau|C)]}_{\text{Pragmatic Value}} - \underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{Q(o_\tau|\pi)}\left[D_{\mathrm{KL}}\left[Q(s_\tau|o_\tau, \pi) \,\|\, Q(s_\tau|\pi)\right]\right]}_{\text{Epistemic Value}}
\end{equation}

\noindent where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $Q(s_\tau|\pi)$ is the prior predictive belief over states given policy $\pi$
    \item $Q(s_\tau|o_\tau, \pi)$ is the posterior belief after observing $o_\tau$
    \item $P(o_\tau|C)$ is the preferred outcome distribution encoding the agent's goals
    \item $D_{\mathrm{KL}}[\cdot \| \cdot]$ is the Kullback--Leibler divergence, measuring expected information gain
\end{itemize}

The pragmatic term drives the agent toward goal-consistent observations. The epistemic term drives the agent toward actions that maximally resolve uncertainty about hidden states. Both terms emerge from the same variational objective; there are no tunable weights balancing exploration against exploitation.

For discrete state-spaces, we compute EFE exactly via Bayesian belief updates over the generative model, following the standard approach in the AIF literature \cite{dacosta2020}.

\subsection{Agent Specifications}

We compare five agents, the first four of which share the same $\rho$-POMDP architecture and differ only in their objective function and planning depth:

\paragraph{Myopic Reward-Maximizing Agent.} Selects actions via one-step lookahead over expected reward. Equivalent to a $\rho$-POMDP with $\rho = 0$, $H = 1$. Serves as the weakest baseline.

\paragraph{Planning Agent.} A multi-step reward-only agent ($\rho = 0$, $H > 1$) using the same recursive tree-search structure as the VFE agent but optimizing only cumulative expected reward---no information gain, no EFE. This baseline controls for planning depth: any difference between VFE and Planning is attributable to the EFE objective, not to deeper lookahead.

\paragraph{Information Gain Agent.} A $\rho$-POMDP agent with $\rho(b) = w \cdot (H(b) - \mathbb{E}[H(b')])$, where $w$ is an environment-specific weight. We report both untuned ($w = 1.0$) and tuned results, where $w$ is selected via grid search over $\{0.1, 0.5, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20, 50, 100\}$ to maximize success rate on 200 tuning episodes.

\paragraph{VFE Agent.} Selects actions by minimizing EFE (Equation 2) over a recursive planning horizon. At each decision point, the agent evaluates:
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{G}(\text{observe}) = c_{\text{obs}} - I(b) + \mathbb{E}_{o}\!\left[\min_a \mathcal{G}(a \mid b'_o)\right]
\end{equation}
where $c_{\text{obs}}$ is the observation cost, $I(b) = H(b) - \mathbb{E}_o[H(b'_o)]$ is the expected information gain at the current belief, and the expectation recurses over posterior beliefs $b'_o$ up to a planning horizon $H$. For commit actions, $\mathcal{G}(\text{commit}_i) = -\mathbb{E}_b[R_i]$, the negative expected reward under the current belief. The agent selects $\arg\min_a \mathcal{G}(a)$. No explicit information-seeking bonus is required; epistemic foraging emerges from the intrinsic information gain term $I(b)$ combined with the recursive evaluation of improved future decisions.

\paragraph{PyMDP Reference Agent.} A wrapper around the \texttt{pymdp} library's standard active inference Agent class \cite{heins2022}, using single-step (myopic) policy evaluation with the same observation model and prior preferences. This provides an independent implementation for validating mathematical consistency of our EFE computation. Both implementations agree on observe-vs-commit decisions across tested belief states.

\section{Experiments}

\subsection{Tiger Problem}

The Tiger problem \cite{kaelbling1998} is a canonical POMDP benchmark that isolates epistemic foraging behavior. An agent faces two doors, behind one of which is a tiger (penalty) and behind the other a reward. The agent can listen (receiving a noisy observation about the tiger's location) or open a door (terminating the episode).

\begin{itemize}
    \item States: $\{$tiger\_left, tiger\_right$\}$
    \item Actions: $\{$listen, open\_left, open\_right$\}$
    \item Observations: $\{$hear\_left, hear\_right$\}$
    \item Listen accuracy: 0.85
    \item Rewards: correct door $+10$, wrong door $-100$, listen cost $-1$
\end{itemize}

The extreme reward asymmetry ($+10$ vs.\ $-100$) makes information gathering critical: committing prematurely is catastrophic. This amplifies behavioral differences between agents with different epistemic strategies.

\subsection{Two-State Information-Seeking Testbed}

A minimal environment with two hidden states, noisy observations (75\% accuracy), and symmetric reward structure (correct $+1$, incorrect $-1$, observation cost $-0.1$). Serves as a simpler validation testbed.

Both environments are implemented as OpenAI Gymnasium environments for standardization and reproducibility.

\subsection{Evaluation Metrics}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Belief Convergence Rate}: Steps to reach a belief confidence threshold; belief entropy reduction curve.
    \item \textbf{Sample Efficiency}: Number of observations required before committing; variance across trials.
    \item \textbf{Policy Quality}: Expected cumulative reward; success rate; statistical significance via t-tests.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Results}

All experiments were run for 1{,}000 episodes per agent (500 for navigation and scaling). Statistical significance was assessed via independent two-sample $t$-tests. Each experiment includes the Planning agent at the same horizon $H$ as VFE, the untuned Information Gain agent ($w = 1.0$), the per-environment tuned Information Gain agent, and the PyMDP reference agent where applicable.

The Tiger problem's extreme reward asymmetry ($+10$ vs.\ $-100$) makes it a test of whether agents gather sufficient evidence before committing. Myopic and untuned Info Gain both listen exactly once and achieve only 84--85\% success with large negative rewards.

This establishes that on Tiger, multi-step reward planning alone suffices to discover the value of listening---the reward structure naturally penalizes premature commitment. Per-environment tuned Info Gain ($w = 20$) performs equivalently.

The PyMDP reference agent, using pymdp's standard single-step active inference, achieves 97.5\% success with only 2.68 listens, confirming that EFE-based exploration is effective even without recursive planning.

The key implication for simple observe-then-commit environments: VFE achieves performance equivalent to a tuned alternative \emph{without requiring per-environment weight selection}.

\subsection{Cross-Environment Summary}

The expanded baselines reveal a more nuanced picture than VFE uniformly dominating:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simple observe-then-commit} (Tiger, testbed): VFE, Planning, and tuned Info Gain achieve equivalent success. VFE's advantage is zero-tuning robustness, not absolute performance.
    \item \textbf{Multi-observation-action environments} (Diagnosis, Bandit): VFE significantly outperforms Planning at the same horizon ($p < 0.05$). EFE's information gain term provides genuine value by guiding \emph{which} observation action to take.
    \item \textbf{Very small spatial environments} (3$\times$3 navigation): VFE over-explores. A greedy agent outperforms because exhaustive search is cheap.
    \item \textbf{Tuned Info Gain}: Consistently achieves high success but over-explores (and the required weight varies from $w=20$ to $w=100$ across environments), demonstrating the fragility that VFE avoids.
\end{itemize}

\section{Hypotheses}

\paragraph{H1 (Tractability) --- Supported.} VFE serves as an effective generalized utility function for $\rho$-POMDPs across all five environments, including multi-action observation spaces (diagnosis with $K$ tests, bandit with $K$ arms) and spatial domains (navigation). Computational cost scales as $\mathcal{O}(K \cdot |\mathcal{O}|^H)$ in the number of observation actions $K$, observation outcomes $|\mathcal{O}|$, and planning horizon $H$, which necessitates modest horizons ($H=2$--$3$) for environments with $K \geq 3$ observation actions. Validation against the \texttt{pymdp} reference implementation confirms mathematical consistency of our EFE computation.

\paragraph{H2 (Epistemic Superiority) --- Supported with Important Qualifications.} The inclusion of a same-horizon Planning baseline and per-environment tuned Info Gain reveals that VFE's advantage is \emph{environment-dependent}. On simple observe-then-commit problems (Tiger, testbed), Planning at the same horizon achieves equivalent success: the reward structure alone provides sufficient gradient for exploration. VFE's genuine advantage appears in multi-observation-action environments (Diagnosis: 97.0\% vs.\ Planning's 87.9\%, $p = 0.022$; Bandit: 88.1\% and $+6.46$ reward vs.\ Planning's 67.5\% and $+5.44$, $p < 0.001$), where the agent must decide \emph{which} information to gather. In these settings, EFE's information gain term provides action-selection guidance that reward-only planning cannot replicate. On very small spatial environments (3$\times$3 navigation), VFE over-explores relative to a greedy baseline. The scaling analysis confirms that VFE's advantage over Planning \emph{grows} with state space size: from $+7.6$ pp at $N=2$ to $+30.3$ pp at $N=16$.

\paragraph{H3 (Constitutive Epistemics) --- Supported, with Boundary Conditions.} The VFE agent exhibits constitutive epistemic drive: it gathers more information than baselines, adapts its exploration depth to environmental structure, and requires no per-environment tuning. Critically, this constitutive epistemics is most valuable when the agent faces \emph{structured uncertainty}---multiple observation modalities, asymmetric information content across actions, or large state spaces. In environments where exhaustive search is cheap (small grids) or reward structure naturally incentivizes exploration (Tiger), the constitutive epistemic drive provides no advantage and can be counterproductive. The tuned Info Gain results further illuminate this: weights that achieve high success on one environment ($w = 20$ for Tiger) are inappropriate on others ($w = 100$ for Diagnosis, $w = 50$ for Bandit), whereas VFE achieves competitive performance across all environments with the same objective function.

\section{Intrinsic Exploration--Exploitation Balancing}

A key theoretical claim of this framework is that the exploration--exploitation trade-off resolves itself dynamically. When the belief state $Q(s)$ has high entropy (high uncertainty), the information gain term $I(b)$ in Equation 3 is large, reducing $\mathcal{G}(\text{observe})$ and favoring information-seeking actions. As observations accumulate and beliefs sharpen, $I(b)$ decays toward zero and the pragmatic commit value dominates, shifting behavior toward goal realization. This transition requires no scheduling, annealing, or hyperparameter adjustment.

Our expanded baselines sharpen this claim. On the Tiger problem, both VFE and the same-horizon Planning agent achieve $\sim$99\% success with $\sim$4.2 listens. Here, the dynamic balance is matched by the reward structure itself: the $+10$ / $-100$ asymmetry makes premature commitment so costly that even a reward-only planner ``discovers'' the value of listening. The VFE mechanism produces the same behavior \emph{for different reasons}---the information gain term, rather than the reward penalty, drives continued listening.

The behavioral equivalence on Tiger obscures a deeper difference that emerges on multi-observation-action environments. On the Bandit ($K=4$ arms), VFE inspects 4.94 times and achieves $+6.46$ reward, while the same-horizon Planning agent inspects 3.26 times for only $+5.44$. The Planning agent lacks guidance on \emph{which} arm to inspect, whereas VFE's information gain term naturally directs inspection toward the most uncertain arms. This demonstrates that intrinsic exploration--exploitation balancing is most valuable when the action space includes multiple information-gathering options with differential informativeness.

The tuned Info Gain results expose a complementary pathology: when the exploration weight is optimized per environment, the agent systematically \emph{over-explores}, achieving 99+\% success but lowest reward on multiple environments. A fixed weight cannot simultaneously be appropriate across environments with different reward scales, demonstrating that additive exploration bonuses are fundamentally mismatched to the problem structure in ways that the EFE objective avoids.

\section{Future Work}

The experiments presented here validate EFE-as-$\rho$ across five structurally distinct environments with state spaces up to $N=16$. Several directions remain:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Continuous state spaces}: Variational message passing or particle filter-based inference to extend beyond discrete state spaces, enabling application to robotics and continuous control.
    \item \textbf{LLM integration}: Using large language models as generative world models to parameterize transition dynamics and observation likelihoods, enabling EFE computation in semantic state spaces.
    \item \textbf{Computational scaling}: The recursive EFE evaluation has exponential cost in the planning horizon. Monte-Carlo tree search or amortized policy networks could enable deeper planning in larger state spaces.
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical generative models}: Nested models with beliefs at multiple levels of abstraction, enabling epistemic foraging over both low-level states and high-level structure.
    \item \textbf{Multi-agent settings}: Whether constitutive epistemics produces beneficial properties (cooperation, communication) when multiple VFE agents interact.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Novelty Statement}

The novelty of this work lies in formally bridging the $\rho$-POMDP and active inference frameworks by substituting Expected Free Energy as the belief-dependent utility function $\rho$, and in rigorously characterizing \emph{where} this substitution helps and where it does not. While $\rho$-POMDPs provide the formal mechanism for belief-dependent optimization and active inference provides the principled objective, neither literature has systematically explored this integration with controlled baselines.

Our contribution is both theoretical---showing the substitution is coherent, tractable, and consistent with the pymdp reference implementation---and empirical. Through comparison against five baselines (Myopic, same-horizon Planning, untuned and tuned Information Gain, and pymdp's standard AIF agent), we establish that: (1) on simple observe-then-commit environments, VFE matches the best tuned alternatives without requiring per-environment weight selection; (2) on multi-observation-action environments requiring \emph{which}-information-to-gather decisions, VFE significantly outperforms same-horizon reward-only planning ($+20.6$ pp on Bandit, $+9.1$ pp on Diagnosis); and (3) on very small state spaces, VFE's constitutive epistemic drive can over-explore. The scaling analysis confirms that VFE's advantage over the Planning baseline \emph{grows} with state space size (from $+7.6$ pp at $N=2$ to $+30.3$ pp at $N=16$), identifying structured uncertainty as the regime where principled epistemic foraging is most valuable.
