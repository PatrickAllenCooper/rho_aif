1. Importance to AI researchers and potential use of the results

Information-gain rewards introduce a weight whose useful value depends on reward units and the task. We study how to calibrate this weight within a specified rho-POMDP policy family to an expected sensing-usage target. A crossing threshold, finite-grid estimate and randomized endpoint mixture are distinguished, with explicit conditions for attaining a target. We establish scale equivariance and conditional stationary convergence of an online controller. The specified recursive, reward-based Expected Free Energy objective identifies a weight-one member under stated units and reward assumptions. Experiments separate target matching from reward maximization under a cap, compare alternative mixtures, and expose irrelevant sensing and transfer failures. Researchers can use the procedure when expected sensing usage is itself a requirement, while retaining separate checks of reward, relevance and robustness.

2. Closest JAIR papers and differences

The closest JAIR articles are (1) Krivic, Cashmore, Magazzeni, and Szedmak, "Using Machine Learning for Decreasing State Uncertainty in Planning" (JAIR 69, 2020), which also decides what to sense before acting, but in classical contingent planning with learned predictors, not a belief-dependent-reward POMDP formalism, and without weight calibration or budget theory. (2) Lim, Becker, Kochenderfer, Tomlin, and Sunberg, "Optimality Guarantees for Particle Belief Approximation of POMDPs" (JAIR 77, 2023), which shares the POMDP-solving concern but addresses continuous-state particle-filter approximation error, not belief-dependent reward weighting. (3) Meli, Castellini, and Farinelli, "Learning Logic Specifications for Policy Guidance in POMDPs" (JAIR 79, 2024), which uses the same RockSample benchmark and POMCP family of solvers but learns interpretable heuristics by inductive logic programming rather than deriving an information-gain weight from a variational objective. These papers do not study calibration of an information-gain weight to an expected sensing-usage target.

3. Prior publication, extensions and prior JAIR submission

Yes. An abridged version of the observe-then-commit equivalence, the two-state threshold characterization, the factored-observation extension, and the EFE benchmark battery (the observe-then-commit environments and RockSample) was accepted as a poster and spotlight presentation at the 7th International Workshop on Active Inference (IWAI 2026), to appear in Springer CCIS proceedings. This submission is a substantially extended version. It adds the entire budgeted rho-POMDP formulation (the usage curve, the operational shadow price with its crossing bracket and endpoint mixture at usage gaps, exact scale equivariance for the receding-horizon planner, monotone comparative statics, and a projected dual controller with stationary convergence under stated conditions and empirically demonstrated re-adaptation under reward-scale shift), the budget experiment battery (usage-curve collapse across reward scales, multi-seed online control under a tenfold reward rescale, cost-denominated budgets, a Structural Inspection benchmark with over 65,000 states, SARSOP and sampled usage-constrained references, and a budget-frontier study at calibration-derived target budgets on held-out seeds), a POMCP comparison on RockSample selected on tuning seeds disjoint from evaluation, and a relevance-weighted information-gain variant that removes reward-irrelevant sensing on a distractor benchmark. It also adds a public recorded-sensor study with learned likelihoods, targets fixed independently of usage curves, direct-penalty and fixed-count controls, and chronological transfer. The study reports both unavailable targets and transfer failures.
